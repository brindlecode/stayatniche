At Norah Head Lighthouse you stay in the lighthouse keepers' quarters, the historic cottages beside the tower, rather than inside the lighthouse itself. The quarters are heritage listed and are rented as holiday accommodation by Reflections Holidays, at 40 Bush Street, Norah Head, on the New South Wales coast.

## The keepers' quarters

There are two quarters to choose from, and each has three bedrooms and one bathroom and sleeps up to nine guests. The Head Lightkeeper's Quarters is a spacious cottage with high ceilings, polished timber floors and breathtaking views. It has a fully equipped kitchen, a separate dining room, a lounge and a wraparound veranda suited to barbecues. The Assistant Lightkeeper's Quarters has the same historic character but a different layout, with a combined kitchen and dining area, a large lounge and a veranda with outdoor seating. Neither accepts dogs.

The size of the cottages makes them a natural fit for a family or a group of friends sharing a coastal holiday.

## The lighthouse next door

The lighthouse was inaugurated on 15 November 1903 and is built from pre-cast concrete blocks made on site. The tower is 27 metres high, with 96 stairs up to the top. It was designed in the style of the colonial architect James Barnet, and it still holds its original lens, a Fresnel design with 700 prisms. The light ran on a kerosene burner until mains electricity arrived in 1961, and it was automated in 1995. Volunteers run tours of the inside every day between 10am and 2pm, so the climb is easy to add to a stay.

On the grounds are a flag locker built to store more than 40 signal flags, a weather station that reports to the Bureau of Meteorology in Sydney, and a rock platform formed 180 to 280 million years ago. A 2.5 km loop walk, graded as a grade 4 hike, takes about an hour and a quarter.
