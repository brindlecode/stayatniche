Stonefield Castle is a castle in the Victorian sense: a grand baronial house rather than a fortress. It was completed in 1837 for John Campbell, to a design by the Edinburgh architect William Henry Playfair, on the site of an older house called Barmore. The Campbells sold it in 1948 and it has been a hotel since 1950. It stands just north of Tarbert, on the Kintyre peninsula, with its gardens running down to Loch Fyne.

## The house

Much of the original interior is still in place: wood panelling, ornate ceilings and marble fireplaces. The building is category B listed. Bedrooms are individually decorated and en suite, with views over the gardens and the loch.

## The gardens

The grounds are as much a reason to come as the house. There are 60 acres of woodland gardens, and they are listed in Scotland's national inventory of gardens and designed landscapes for their plant collection. John Campbell planted rhododendrons and magnolias here from seed brought back from botanical expeditions around 1850, and later generations of the family added to them. Playfair also designed bridges and a folly in the grounds.

## Eating

The Barmore Bistro looks across the loch and concentrates on Scottish produce, with local seafood a particular feature.

## Practical notes

The hotel is two miles from Tarbert, a fishing village. Parking is free, and dogs are welcome for an extra charge.
