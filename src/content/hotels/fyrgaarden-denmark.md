Fyrgaarden is the old lighthouse keepers' house on the Danish island of Anholt, in the Kattegat Sea. Guests stay in the keeper's house next to the old lighthouse, not inside the lighthouse itself. The house was once home to the keeper and his family, and it stands among turquoise water and sand dunes, with no neighbours ahead of it.

## A house without electricity

Life here is deliberately simple. There is no electricity, so oil lamps light the rooms once the sun goes down and the fridges and stoves run on gas. Water is pumped by hand from the well in the middle of the courtyard, and there is also a pump in each apartment. The toilets are in the outhouse. Rooms are full of daylight, and visitors receive a practical guide when they book, because staying here asks a little more thought than a typical holiday.

## The apartments

The house is divided into three separate apartments, each with a kitchen and several bedrooms. You can rent the whole house or just one apartment. Apartment 1 takes up to 15 people, while Apartments 2 and 3 each take up to 7. The house has a long tradition as a place where people gather across generations, eating together in the garden or finding quiet by the dunes and the sea.

## Getting there and booking

The easiest way to reach the house is to drive along the beach towards the lighthouse, and a car suited to beach driving can be rented during your stay. High-season weeks run from 25 June to 13 August, and booking for the high season opens on 1 March. Enquiries for the rest of the year can be sent at any time.

## An artist residency

Alongside the accommodation, Fyrgaarden runs an artist residency. The setting is peaceful and quiet, surrounded by nature, and suits anyone wanting a break from connected everyday life.
