Ashford Castle has origins in the 13th century, evolving from a medieval fortress into a grand retreat. It was once home to the Guinness family, and the result is 350 acres of Irish estate with a castle at its centre that is unambiguously the real thing: not a Victorian folly, not a hotel that happens to have a turret.

The 83 rooms and suites, including a historic boathouse perched over the lake, are individually designed. Many look out over Lough Corrib, which at 68 square kilometres is Ireland's second-largest lake, stretching to the horizon in a view that makes sitting in a window seat with a whiskey feel like a moral obligation.

Falconry is one of the experiences on the estate, with experts to guide you and no previous experience necessary. Beyond that, the activity list runs to sea trout and salmon fishing on Lough Corrib, clay pigeon shooting, archery, horse riding, and boat trips on the lake. The George V Dining Room was built by the Guinness family in honour of the 1905 visit of the Prince of Wales, who became George V.

County Mayo is one of the more dramatically beautiful parts of Ireland. Connemara is on the doorstep, the islands of Clew Bay are within reach, and the village of Cong, where John Ford filmed most of The Quiet Man on the castle estate in 1951, is worth an hour of wandering. Ashford is one of the few castle hotels that would justify Ireland as a destination on its own.
