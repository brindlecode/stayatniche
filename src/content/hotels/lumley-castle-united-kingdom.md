Lumley Castle is a four-square medieval castle at Chester-le-Street, near the city of Durham, on the banks of the Lumley Park Burn. It has been a hotel since the 1970s, and it is still the property of the Earl of Scarbrough, whose family built it.

## History

The castle takes its name from Sir Ralph Lumley, who turned his family's manor house into a castle in 1389 after returning from the wars in Scotland. He was later executed for his part in a plot against Henry IV and the castle was forfeited, returning to his grandson in 1421.

In 1603 James VI of Scotland stopped here on his way south to take the English throne. He did not stay the night, but the King James Suite, in what was once the castle chapel, is named after the visit. The Georgian alterations are attributed to Sir John Vanbrugh, among them the library, which is now the Black Knight Restaurant.

In the 19th century the castle became a residence of the Bishop of Durham and then a hall of residence for University College, Durham, whose students spent their first year here. The college sold it in the 1960s, and the conversion to a hotel followed in 1976.

## Ghost stories

Lumley has a reputation as one of the most haunted places in County Durham. The best-known tale is of Lily of Lumley, said to have been thrown down a well in the grounds. Visiting cricket teams staying at the castle have added their own accounts, and an escape game in the old beer cellar, opened in 2019, is built around the story.

## Setting

The castle overlooks Durham County Cricket Club's Riverside Ground and is the backdrop to matches there. The building is Grade I listed.

## Practical notes

Rooms differ a good deal in a building of this age, so ask which part of the castle a room is in before you book.
