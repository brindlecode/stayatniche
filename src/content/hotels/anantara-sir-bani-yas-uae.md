Sir Bani Yas Island is a study in what political will and ecological investment can achieve. In 1977, the late Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan — the founding president of the UAE — banned hunting on the island and began developing it as a wildlife reserve, part of his "Greening of the Desert" programme to provide a haven for endangered Arabian wildlife. The reserve now spans over 87 square kilometres and is home to thousands of large free-roaming animals, including Arabian oryx, gazelle and giraffe; more than 10,000 animals from the island have been released into reserves elsewhere in Abu Dhabi, such as the one in the Liwa Desert.

The result is a safari experience: game drives and nature trails in the Arabian Wildlife Park, past Arabian oryx — the animal that may have given rise to the unicorn legend, its single-horn profile distinctive at distance — alongside giraffe (introduced for diversity), cheetah, striped hyena, and more than 100 species of wild birds. The guides are specialist wildlife staff, not hotel concierges; the information they carry about the individual animals' histories and behaviours reflects years of daily contact with the same population.

Three Anantara hotels, all operated by Minor Hotels, sit on the island: Desert Islands Resort & Spa by Anantara in the northwest (opened 2008, 64 rooms), Anantara Sir Bani Yas Island Al Sahel Villa Resort in the west and Anantara Sir Bani Yas Island Al Yamm Villa Resort in the east (both opened 2013, 30 villas each). They are separate properties with their own room types, so confirm which one you are booking and check its current opening status and availability with the hotel. The island offers game drives, nature trails, mountain biking and kayaking, and the combination of wildlife, activities and cultural context (the island also holds a 7th-century Christian church, opened to the public in 2010 and the first pre-Islamic Christian site found in the UAE) makes for an unusually rich short-break proposition.

Access is by ferry from Jebel Dhanna terminal (2 hours from Abu Dhabi) or by seaplane from Abu Dhabi. A private road-and-ferry transfer from Dubai is approximately 4 hours.
