Cambodia's Cardamom Mountains stretch across the country's southwest, a 4.4-million-hectare rainforest that represents the largest remaining intact forest in mainland Southeast Asia. It is home to more than 50 endangered animal species, including elephants, Malayan sun bears, pangolins, clouded leopards and pileated gibbons — species threatened across the region by poaching, logging and other illegal activities. The forest is not an attraction in the conventional sense; it is a functioning ecosystem whose persistence depends on the decision that it is worth more intact than logged.

Shinta Mani Wild takes this conservation premise and turns it into the product itself. The resort helps fund Wildlife Alliance, a Cambodian NGO whose armed rangers patrol the Cardamom forest, and has built an on-site ranger station for them on its 350-hectare (865-acre) property; it says it pays all of the rangers' expenses and equipment. Guests can join the rangers on motorbikes for patrols of two to six hours, depending on stamina, which puts them on the front lines of the conservation work their stay helps fund.

The arrival is pure theatre. The camp is reached by zipline — a 400-metre cable above a rainforest river — or by a longer forest walk. The zipline experience, taken at tree canopy level above rushing water, is an exhilarating flight over the treetops. The 15 tents, designed by architect Bill Bensley, are set along the river, stilted, secluded and air-conditioned, each with a vast porch and an outdoor tub. The design is deliberately extravagant — patterned fabrics, layered textures, deliberately maximalist — as a counterpoint to the minimalist rainforest outside.

Phnom Penh is the nearest international gateway; from there, Koh Kong is 4 hours by road, and the camp is a further 2-hour boat journey up the Preak Tachan River. The resort provides full transfer logistics.
