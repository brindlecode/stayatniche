Alila Villas Uluwatu sits on a large clifftop plot on Bali's Bukit Peninsula, and the site is the whole argument. The peninsula is limestone clifftops above legendary surf, home to one of the island's most significant Hindu temples, and far enough from Seminyak's noise to feel like a different Bali. The cliff-edge villas have uninterrupted ocean views; the hillside villas climb the slope in tiers, and many of them see little of the sea from the room.

WOHA, the Singapore architects, designed the resort as a series of low cream-rendered walls that reveal themselves gradually as you move deeper into the property. The reveal of the 50-metre cliffside infinity pool on first approach is calculated and it works. Most of the public spaces are designed around that view.

Each villa is a private compound with its own private pool. The architecture uses rough limestone, polished terrazzo, and hand-woven textiles, with large sliding glass doors that open fully to the sea breeze. Air conditioning is largely unnecessary: the clifftop position generates enough cross-ventilation that the breezes from the Indian Ocean do the job. The design is consistent and confident throughout, no jarring notes, no excess.

Sustainability at Alila isn't performative. Rainwater harvesting, greywater recycling, an organic garden supplying the AWOL restaurant, and passive cooling design are all measurable commitments. The AWOL menu uses Balinese culinary foundations and produce from across the Indonesian archipelago. The wine list rewards attention.

The surf at Uluwatu, Padang Padang, and Bingin is accessible by cliff path. The Kecak fire dance at Uluwatu Temple at sunset, staged on a clifftop with the ocean as a backdrop, is one of the more genuinely spectacular things available in Bali. This is a hotel that earns the location it occupies.
