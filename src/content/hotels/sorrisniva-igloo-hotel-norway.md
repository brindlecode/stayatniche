Sorrisniva Igloo Hotel is built each winter on the frozen banks of the Alta River at 70 degrees north, and the location does serious work. Alta sits deep in the Finnmark plateau, the northernmost city in Norway of any significant size, and its Kp index readings regularly reach the threshold at which northern lights are not merely possible but reliable. This is one of the best aurora positions in Europe, with a proper hotel built around it rather than just a marketing claim attached to it.

The igloo hotel is rebuilt from scratch every winter and is open each year from 20 December to 31 March. Construction takes about five weeks and is done by local craftsmen, using fresh-water ice cut from a nearby mountain lake and snow made from Alta River water: about 250 tonnes of ice and 7,000 cubic metres of snow for roughly 2,500 square metres of hotel. The structure is built layer by layer and then fitted out with ice furniture, sculptures and ice art, so the rooms are never the same from one year to the next. Each year has a theme drawn from local history or mythology, such as the Vikings, Nordic legends or local wildlife. The hotel has more than 20 rooms and suites, together with an ice chapel, an ice bar and ice sculptures.

Sleeping in the ice suites is the heart of a stay at Sorrisniva. The beds are covered with reindeer skins, and each guest gets two Arctic sleeping bags, each rated to withstand -30°C. The temperature inside the hotel is a steady -4 to -7°C: cold enough to be genuinely atmospheric, comfortable enough to sleep. Snow absorbs sound, so the rooms are extremely quiet, and the hotel itself describes the deep silence and serenity of the ice as part of the experience.

The surrounding Alta landscape offers further experiences. In Alta, the World Heritage Rock Art Centre - Alta Museum presents the Rock Art of Alta, a UNESCO World Heritage site listed in 1985 where more than 6,000 carvings have been found. The Igloo Hotel is the starting point for husky sledding, snowmobiling across the Finnmark plateau, snowshoeing, northern lights hunting and Sámi experiences. Alta Airport is served by direct flights from Oslo, making access straightforward despite the latitude.
