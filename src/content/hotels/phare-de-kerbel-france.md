The Phare de Kerbel in Riantec, Brittany, was built in 1913 to guide ships into the harbour of Lorient. It stopped working as a light in August 1989. Today, according to its owners, it is the only lighthouse in France that can be lived in at its summit, where a studio now stands in place of the lantern, 25 metres up.

## What is where

- **In the tower:** the studio at the top. The old lantern was replaced by a glazed room with a 360-degree panorama over the sea, and every pane has a blackout blind. It has a kitchenette with a small fridge, two hotplates, a microwave, a few pots and pans, crockery and cutlery. Bed and bath linen are provided there, and a breakfast for the first morning is left for guests.
- **The oil room:** a former storeroom, used for the oil that fuelled the lens until the lighthouse was electrified in 1932. It is now an outbuilding where guests can spend a night at the foot of the lighthouse.
- **The keeper's house:** attached to the lighthouse, it was the keepers' home from 1913 until the light went out in 1989. It has been completely redone for comfort while keeping the spirit of its first occupants.

There is no lift. Guests climb the 126 steps to the top, as the keepers did. There is no Wi-Fi.

## Garden, pool and sauna

The garden covers more than 1,000 square metres, with garden furniture for breakfast and sun loungers by the pool. Guests are alone on the property and have the pool and sauna to themselves. The sauna is open all year, and the pool is heated and open from early May to late September. There are three private parking spaces and an outdoor socket for charging an electric car. Dogs are not accepted.

## The keeper

Honorine Le Guen took up her post here in 1925, aged 23, and kept it until she retired in 1972. She was the first woman lighthouse keeper in France.

## Getting there

The nearest destination for train travellers is Lorient, about 30 minutes away by car. Port-Louis and its Vauban citadel are close by, and the restaurant Avel Vor is five minutes away on foot. A host welcomes each guest on arrival, so tell the lighthouse when you will arrive.
