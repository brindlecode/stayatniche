OaseWeil is a treehouse hotel tucked between forest and meadows inside the Schönbuch Nature Park, close to Weil im Schönbuch in Baden-Württemberg. The village of treehouses has eight of them, all different from one another, all built from solid timber and all heated.

## The treehouses

The property calls its accommodation Wipfelsuiten, or treetop suites. Each one has queen-size beds, a living and dining area, a kitchen and a bathroom with shower and WC, plus its own terrace and a furnished balcony looking over the valley, the meadows and the woods. Six of the houses sleep up to four people: Flaschengeist, Windhexe, Burggespenst, Wipfelträumer, Wetterfee and Lichtelfe. Two, Zeitgeist and Klabautermann, sleep up to six.

The kitchens come with a kettle, two hobs, a small fridge, crockery, cutlery and cooking utensils, along with coffee, tea, salt, pepper, oil and vinegar. Anyone who prefers not to bring supplies can buy ingredients from the shop at reception. Bathrooms have a hairdryer, towels and vegan-friendly shower gel, the living rooms have a TV and telephone, and WiFi covers the treehouses and the outdoor areas. Non-slip slippers made for the treehouses are included, and guests take them home afterwards.

## Hotel service

Despite the woodland setting, the stay works like a hotel. Housekeeping and a reception team look after guests, and each morning a breakfast is served that is vegetarian and largely regional, with eggs and cheese included. Arrival starts with a welcome drink and a personal check-in that goes all the way up to the treehouse. Check-in runs from 15:00 to 17:30 and check-out is by 11:00.

## Around the grounds

The main house holds Ayurveda massage and treatment rooms, and the Schönbuchhalle on the same grounds hosts private and business events. Beyond the property, a 560 km network of marked trails crosses the Schönbuch. The treehouse village is also a base for horse riding, golf, a falconry visit, brand-name warehouse sales, a thermal bath, e-bike hire and guided tours of the nature park.

Rooms are booked online, by phone or by email, and every treehouse has its own name, so ask which one fits your group size.
