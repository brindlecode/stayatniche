Yuquiyú is a rustic retreat in the foothills of El Yunque, Puerto Rico's rainforest, and its accommodation is a set of three artisan-built treehouses. They are not perched in a single tree. Each one stands on pillars among the trees, on the side of a canyon, in an outcropping of massive boulders. Terraces with an organic garden, fruit trees and free parking sit above; a cascading river runs below, with the rainforest beyond.

## The treehouses

The design takes its cue from the small wooden houses that were common across Puerto Rico more than a century ago. Each treehouse has hot and cold running water, electricity, a queen-size bed, a kitchenette and a bath with a slate interior, high-end fixtures and a rain shower. Every one is designed for just two guests, both aged 18 or over.

**El Mirador** (The Lookout) is named for its view of the rainforest peaks. It has 144 square feet inside and an 84-square-foot balcony, plus a small side garden. The private bath is on the main level and is reached by a footbridge from the balcony.

**La Cara de Indio** (The Indian Face) takes its name from a Taino petroglyph not far from the property. It also has 144 square feet inside and an 84-square-foot balcony, set among palms, mango trees and tropical plants. The 90-square-foot private bath sits below the main level, down a short gravel path.

**La Cascada** (The Cascade) is named for the river below and sits in a grove of Royal Palms. It has 121 square feet inside and the largest balcony of the three, at 176 square feet. Its 81-square-foot private bath is below the main level, reached by a stairway from the balcony.

## On the property

Guests can use a pool with a view and a plunge pool for cooling off. The property is less than an hour from San Juan's airport.

The treehouses are compact, and in each the private bath is reached by a footbridge, a gravel path or a stairway rather than from inside the sleeping space.
