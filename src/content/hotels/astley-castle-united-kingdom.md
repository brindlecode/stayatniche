Astley Castle is an unusual place to stay, and not only because it is old. It is a ruin with new rooms built inside it. The moated manor in North Warwickshire burned out in 1978 and stood derelict for three decades. Instead of rebuilding what was lost, the restoration set modern rooms within the surviving walls, and the building reopened as a holiday let in 2012. A year later it won the RIBA Stirling Prize for architecture, as an exceptional example of joining an ancient monument to modern architecture.

## Castle or manor?

Astley was never a true castle. The Astley family held the manor from the 12th century and received a licence to fortify the house in 1266, but it remained a fortified manor house. Most of what survives dates from 1555, when the Grey family rebuilt it, or later.

## Three queens

The Greys put Astley at the centre of national events. Sir John Grey's widow, Elizabeth Woodville, became queen to Edward IV. Her daughter Elizabeth of York married Henry VII. And Lady Jane Grey, proclaimed queen in 1553 and executed the following year, was the daughter of Henry Grey of Astley. After the family's disgrace the house was partly demolished and sold. In the English Civil War it held a small Parliamentary garrison. From 1674 it belonged to the Newdigates of nearby Arbury Hall.

## The restoration

The house was leased out in the 20th century and became a hotel in the early 1960s, until the fire. The scheme that saved it was drawn up by the owners with English Heritage, the local authority and the Landmark Trust, and designed by Witherford Watson Mann Architects. The building is Grade II* listed and the site is a Scheduled Ancient Monument.

## Practical notes

Astley is let as a holiday home rather than run as a hotel. It is in North Warwickshire, near Nuneaton.
