The Saugerties Lighthouse is an 1869 beacon on the Hudson River that doubles as a living museum and a bed and breakfast. Overnight guests sleep in the lighthouse itself, on a remote shoal surrounded by water, in an experience that is deliberately simple and nineteenth-century in feel.

## Getting there

The lighthouse is reached on foot along a gentle half-mile trail. The Hudson is tidal here, so check-in is scheduled to avoid high tide and let guests arrive with dry feet and in daylight. The keeper briefs guests on the tides, waterproof boots are available if the path is wet, and a flashlight is needed on the unlit trail after dark. Along the trail you may see beavers, eagles and herons.

## The rooms

There are two second-floor bedrooms, each with a double bed. The rooms are not rented separately, so the whole lighthouse is shared by one party, whether a couple or a group of friends or family. The West Room looks south down the Hudson and west towards the Catskills. The larger East Room looks east onto the river and has its own auxiliary stove for the coldest months. For families, cots for two extra people are available on request. Children are welcome, and must be supervised by a responsible adult at all times.

All linens are provided. The first-floor bathroom is the only one and is shared by everyone staying. It has a sink and a claw-foot basin shower, and a composting toilet is used because of the remote location.

## Life in the lighthouse

The operational tower is open to overnight guests throughout their stay, by way of the original staircase and a ladder to the lantern room. A first-floor parlor has windows all round, a table, binoculars, playing cards and some books, and an old-fashioned stove that the keeper tends. A small museum on the second floor holds the original beehive Fresnel lens and a model of the lighthouse that stood here in 1835.

There are no televisions, and Wi-Fi comes by underwater cable and is weak and intermittent. There is no air-conditioning in summer, only a vintage fan, and the building is heated in winter. A hearty breakfast is included. For other meals, guests can bring food to use the mini-fridge and the outdoor propane grill on the riverside deck.

## Booking

The bed and breakfast is open Thursday to Sunday nights all year. Reservations are taken by phone rather than online, so that the tides and the half-mile walk can be discussed. Sunday nights and Thursday nights are the easiest to reserve.
