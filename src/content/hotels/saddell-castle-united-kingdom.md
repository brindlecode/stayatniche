Saddell Castle is a 16th-century castle on the shore of the Kilbrannan Sound at Saddell, in Kintyre. It is not a hotel. The Landmark Trust bought and restored the castle and now rents it out as a self-catering property.

## History

There was a castle at Saddell in the 12th century, in the time of Somerled. The present one was built between 1508 and 1512 by David Hamilton, Bishop of Argyll, using stone from the earlier castle and from the ruined Saddell Abbey nearby. In the 1550s it passed to James MacDonald of Dunnyveg in exchange for his lands on Arran, and it became one of the MacDonalds' family seats.

In October 1558 an English force under the Earl of Sussex captured and burnt the castle in retaliation for MacDonald's campaigns in Ireland. The MacDonald lairds rebuilt and enlarged it. A trap door in the entrance passage is said to have dropped unwelcome visitors into a dungeon with no way out. In 1607 the Kintyre lands were granted to the Earl of Argyll, and when Saddell House was built around 1774 the castle was left to decay until the Landmark Trust took it on.

## Setting

Saddell Bay is on the Kintyre peninsula, in the Campbeltown area. It is known to many who have never been there: the video for the Wings song "Mull of Kintyre" was filmed on this beach, with the Campbeltown Pipe Band marching along the shore.

## Practical notes

The castle is let self-catering rather than by the room, so it suits a family or a group of friends. Members of Clan Donald still visit Saddell every year for its place in their history.
