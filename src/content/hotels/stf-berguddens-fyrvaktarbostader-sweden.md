At STF Berguddens Fyrvaktarbostäder, guests stay in the lighthouse keepers' dwellings next to Bergudden lighthouse, not inside the lighthouse itself. The dwellings stand on Holmön, the main island of the Holmöarna archipelago off Umeå, at the end of the road beside the rocks and the sea. The lighthouse has warned and guided ships through the uncertain waters of Norra Kvarken for over 130 years, and keepers lived and worked here with their families. Today you can live the keeper's life on this exposed headland, watching the sun go down over the sea, the fog roll in, or the lighthouse beam move on the water.

## The dwellings

There are five rooms with 11 to 25 beds in all. Some rooms have at least four beds, which suits families, and a limited number of rooms accept pets. Guests share a kitchen and cook for themselves. The remote position means plain conditions: there is no internet and no running water, the toilet is an outhouse, and washing is done in the sea or with a basin and jug. The accommodation's own description calls this simplicity another kind of luxury. Check-in is from 13:00 and check-out is by 11:00.

## The island

Holmön is the only island in the Västerbotten archipelago with residents all year. The village centre is well preserved and, with the surrounding landscape, is classed as a national interest area. The Holmöarna also contain one of Sweden's largest marine nature reserves. A marked walking trail and a swimming spot are both within 1 km of the dwellings, and guides can be hired.

## Getting there

From Umeå, drive north on the E4, leave at the northern Sävar exit and follow signs to Norrfjärden; the drive takes 30 to 40 minutes, and parking at the ferry is free. In summer the ferry Capella runs from Norrfjärden to Byviken five times a day, a crossing of 40 minutes. When the winter ice is thick enough, a hovercraft takes over, weather and ice permitting. From Byviken it is 3 km to Bergudden. There are no taxis on Holmön, so bring a bicycle on the ferry or hire one from the Holmön boat museum.
