Raja Ampat translates as "Four Kings" — a reference to the four main islands of an archipelago in Southwest Papua that sits at the heart of the Coral Triangle, the global centre of marine biodiversity. The numbers are staggering: over 1,500 species of fish, 700 species of molluscs, and 600 species of coral — more than the entire Caribbean — compressed into the waters around this island chain. Marine biologists who have spent careers studying reef systems describe Raja Ampat's dive sites as unlike anything else they have encountered.

Misool Eco Resort is a private island resort in Raja Ampat, reached via Sorong, the regional gateway, in a remote sector of the archipelago. Its accommodation is eight Water Cottages built on stilts over the North Lagoon, four slightly larger North Lagoon Villas in the same area, seven South Beach Villas overlooking a blue water swimming hole, and the secluded Villa Rajawali.

The resort's no-take marine reserve has been the subject of scientific study. A 2022 publication in Frontiers in Marine Science documented the recovery of coral and fish populations within the reserve boundary compared to surrounding areas — including species and population densities that had been lost from the broader region. The resort employs rangers to patrol the reserve against illegal fishing boats; guests fund this directly through their stay fees.

Above water, the karst limestone towers of Raja Ampat — rising hundreds of metres from the sea in characteristic mushroom shapes — are navigable by kayak through sea caves and mangrove channels. Birdwatchers come for the endemic Red and Wilson's Birds-of-Paradise, accessible by early morning guided hike to nearby islands. Access is by liveaboard transfer from Sorong, which is reached by direct flight from Makassar, Manado, or Jakarta.
