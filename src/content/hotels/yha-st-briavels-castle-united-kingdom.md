This is a hostel, not a hotel, and that is the point: St Briavels is a budget way to spend a night inside a real medieval castle. The building stands on a spur above the River Wye on the western edge of the Forest of Dean, behind a large gatehouse and a moat that is now a garden.

## The castle

St Briavels was built between 1075 and 1129 as the royal administrative centre for the Forest of Dean. In the 13th century it was a favourite hunting lodge of King John, and then England's main centre for making arrows and crossbow bolts. Later it served as a court and as a debtors' prison, whose conditions the reformer John Howard recorded in 1775. After a restoration around 1900 it became a youth hostel in 1948 and has been one ever since. The castle is Grade I listed and owned by English Heritage.

## Where you sleep

There are eight bedrooms sleeping about 64 people in all, mostly in bunks, with a few single beds and double beds under high sleepers. Rooms sleep from 5 to 14 people. Three of them are in the east tower, sleeping 6, 8 and 10. You can book a bed in a shared room, a private room, or the whole hostel for a group. Bed linen is provided; towels are not, though they can be rented.

## Shared rooms

Guests cook for themselves in a ground-floor kitchen. The Old Stables dining room opens onto a courtyard, and the Banquet Hall upstairs, reached by a steep staircase, seats 50. The Chapel Lounge on the first floor has a high-beamed ceiling. Original features include wood panelling, an old fireplace and a dog-driven spit wheel.

## Practical notes

Check-in is from 5pm to 10pm and check-out is before 10am. Guests under 16 must stay with an adult.
