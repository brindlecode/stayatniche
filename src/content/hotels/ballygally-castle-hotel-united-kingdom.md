Ballygally Castle stands at the head of Ballygally Bay on the Antrim coast, about three miles north of Larne, with the coast road and the Irish Sea directly in front of it. The castle was built in 1625 by James Shaw, a Scot from Greenock, and it is still in use as a residence. Today it is a four-star hotel run by Hastings Hotels, who bought it in 1966.

## The castle

The old part is a rectangular tower in the Scottish baronial style: four storeys, walls about a metre and a half thick, corner turrets and a stone spiral stair. Over the main door is the Middle Scots inscription "Godis Providens is my Inheritans". The castle was attacked several times during the rebellion of 1641 and never taken. Later it served for a time as a coastguard station before it became a hotel in the 1950s.

## Rooms

The hotel has 54 bedrooms. Most are in later extensions, including the Coastal Deluxe rooms added in 2014, which look straight over the bay; Superior rooms face the walled gardens. If you came for the castle itself, book one of the small number of Tower Bedrooms, which are in the original 17th-century building. At the top, in one of the turrets, is the "ghost room", said to be the bedroom of Lady Isabella Shaw, whose ghost is part of the castle's story. There are also Junior Suites, family and interconnecting rooms, and two fully accessible bedrooms with wet rooms.

## Setting

Ballygally sits on the Causeway Coastal Route, so the hotel works as a base for a drive along the coast. The Gobbins cliff path, the Carrick-a-Rede rope bridge and the Giant's Causeway are the stops the hotel itself points guests to.

## Practical notes

Check-in is from 4pm and check-out is by 11am. Only a few rooms are in the old tower, so ask for a Tower Bedroom by name when you book.
