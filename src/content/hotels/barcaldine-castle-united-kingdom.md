Barcaldine Castle is a small Scottish tower house that you can sleep in. It stands on the south shore of Loch Creran, nine miles north of Oban, and it is run as a bed and breakfast rather than a hotel: a room and breakfast in a building that is four centuries old.

## The castle

Sir Duncan Campbell of Glenorchy, known as Black Duncan, built the castle as a military outpost, completing it by 1609 (the castle's own pages give the start as 1590 on one page and 1601 on another), and it is still called the Black Castle. It follows the L plan common to tower houses of the period. The walls are about nine feet thick and built of local stone, and the castle keeps two secret stairs, a dungeon and the original yett, the iron gate that protected the door.

The Campbells of Barcaldine left in 1735 for a new mansion in the village, and the castle decayed until Sir Duncan Campbell restored it between 1897 and 1906. The outer stonework is original; the turrets and the interior from the great hall upwards date from that restoration. The family sold the castle in 2009 to its present owner.

## Staying

Guests book rooms on a bed and breakfast basis. The castle can also be taken as a whole for exclusive use, and weddings are held in the great hall.

## Setting

From the castle the view runs across Loch Creran to the mountains of Glencoe. Oban, with its ferries to Mull, Iona, Kerrera and Lismore, is a short drive south.

## Getting there

The castle is just off the A828 Oban to Fort William road, 1.5 miles from Benderloch: turn off at the school in Benderloch and follow the brown signs. Glasgow Airport is about two hours' drive away. There are two chargers for electric cars. The owners ask guests to check the availability of the castle and its rooms on their calendar before travelling.
